    Organizer guide¶

    This guide is for organizers running the event on the admin site. It follows the order of the season: set up, open signup, approve and schedule volunteers, send schedules, then run the event days.

    The admin site is at the admin hostname, behind a sign-in gate. Its toolbar has three menus: Call for Volunteers, Admin, and your own name on the right, with Profile and Sign Out. Below the toolbar are three tabs: Schedule, Volunteers and Radios.

    Roles¶

    Role For Can do
    organizer the volunteer team everything
    checkin the check-in desk and radio table check volunteers in, rate them, issue and return radios
    view anyone who only needs to look read only

    First-time setup¶

    A fresh install opens the setup wizard:

    1. Create the admin account, the first organizer. This step locks once any account exists.
    2. Sign in.
    3. Your conference: its name, and how volunteers can reach you: a contact email, the conference website and its code of conduct. Then the logo. The volunteer pages show all of them, and leave out anything blank. Everything can be skipped and changed later in Admin → App Settings.
    4. Set up the schedule. Either build it in the wizard (days, then categories, then times, then a grid of how many people each position needs at each time) or import a CSV.
    5. Import volunteers from a CSV, or skip this and let them sign up.

    Then, before opening signup:

    • Admin → Email Key: upload the organizers' public key. Signup can't open without it. Secrets explains the key pair.
    • Admin → App Settings: set the event timezone, which check-in uses to judge early or late.
    • Admin → Users: add the other organizers, or use Invite Someone to send a link that lets them make their own account. Invite links last 72 hours.

    Editing the schedule¶

    Edit Schedule on the Schedule tab opens the editor:

    • Categories: their order and chip colors.
    • Days: each day, its date, and which times it runs.
    • Times: each time range, and the days it runs on.
    • Grid: how many of each position at each day and time. Changes are applied as a difference. A slot with a volunteer assigned is never removed; the editor lists it instead.

    Signup¶

    Open Call for Volunteers → Volunteer Portal:

    • Volunteer signup open/closed is the switch. Signup also shows as closed while there's no email key, and as full when no two timeslots with space remain.
    • Overbook by lets more volunteers pick a timeslot than it has slots, to make up for people who drop out. Volunteers see the inflated space. Shift Demand shows the real numbers.
    • Early access invites let one person sign up while signup is closed. Make a link to send yourself, or use Send invites by email. Each link works once.
    • The sync status shows when the portal last answered, and any error. Sync now runs a pass at once.

    What the volunteer pages say¶

    The same dialog has three more tabs for the words volunteers see. Each has Save and Reset to default, and the portal has a saved change within a few seconds.

    • Landing page: the public call for volunteers. It's three Markdown parts (the intro above the two buttons, the main column and the sidebar) plus the opportunity cards, each with an icon, a name and a line of text. {{ conference }}, {{ contact_email }}, {{ conference_url }} and {{ code_of_conduct_url }} fill in from App Settings, and {% if contact_email %}…{% endif %} leaves a sentence out when it's blank. Preview shows it as volunteers will see it. Raw HTML, images and links other than web pages and email addresses are left out.
    • Questionnaire: the signup form's questions, in order. For each one, choose its type (choice, dropdown, checkbox, acknowledgement or text), the signup step it's on, whether it's required, and whether volunteers can change their answer later. Choices can ask a follow-up when picked. You can change the questions at any time. Removing one keeps the answers already given, and the editor tells you how many there are first. The flexible question is used by the assignment picker: you can reword or remove it, but it stays a checkbox. Don't ask for anything sensitive, because answers are stored as plain text.
    • Invite email: the subject and body of the early-access invite. It's a template, with {{ first_name }}, {{ conference }}, {{ link }}, {{ expires }} and the App Settings values, plus {% if %}…{% else %}…{% endif %}. The body must include {{ link }}. Preview fills in an invented volunteer.

    Call for Volunteers → Shift Demand shows, for each timeslot, how many slots exist, how many are open, how many volunteers picked it, and how much space is left.

    Approving volunteers¶

    New signups wait at the top of the Volunteers tab, under Pending Volunteers, with their picks, their answers, and any existing volunteer whose name looks similar.

    • Approve makes them active, so they can be assigned. You can give them a badge number as their id at the same time.
    • Make inactive declines them. They stay on file, so the same address can't simply sign up again.

    Approval doesn't schedule anyone. Picks stay preferences until you assign them.

    The volunteer list¶

    The rest of the Volunteers tab lists everyone, whatever their status, with their picks, answers and shifts.

    • Make inactive and Reinstate take someone off the schedule pickers and put them back. Their assignments stay. While they're inactive, those slots show as needing a replacement.
    • Decrypt emails asks for the private key, pasted or chosen from a file, and adds an Email column. Addresses are decrypted in this browser only. The key isn't sent anywhere, and Forget key clears it from the page.
    • Add Volunteer adds someone by hand. Their address is encrypted in the browser before it's sent.
    • Purge deletes an inactive volunteer outright, here and on the portal. Use it to clear test volunteers, so their addresses can sign up again from scratch. Purge inactive deletes every inactive volunteer at once. You type the volunteer's id, or the count, to confirm. It can't be undone: their picks, notes, check-ins and ratings go with them, and their slots become empty.

    Volunteers manage their own registration in the portal. They can change their shifts and some answers, cancel, and rejoin. A cancelled volunteer's slots show as needing a replacement.

    Assigning volunteers¶

    On the Schedule tab, pick a day and a time, then click a slot. The dialog suggests volunteers in groups: those who requested that timeslot, those who said they're flexible, those who are available, and the rest. Anyone already working an overlapping shift that day is left out.

    The banner above the schedule lists what needs attention:

    • Slots needing a replacement, because their volunteer cancelled or was made inactive. Open goes to the slot.
    • Dropped shifts: a volunteer changed their picks in the portal and no longer wants a shift they're assigned to. Unassign them, or Keep the assignment after you've talked to them.

    Sending schedules¶

    1. Call for Volunteers → Schedule Email: write the message in Markdown, add images, and preview it for any volunteer with their real shifts and QR codes.
    2. Download package.
    3. Send with vol_mail from your own machine. See vol_mail and tools/vol_mail/README.md. Usually that's a draft round, then a final round to the people whose shifts changed.

    vol_mail needs an export API key, made under Admin → API Keys with the volunteers:read, volunteers:email and checkin:codes scopes. A key is shown once, so save it straight away.

    App Settings → Rotate key voids every check-in code already sent. Only use it if codes have leaked, and send the schedules again afterwards.

    At the event¶

    Check-in. Check In Volunteer on the Schedule tab opens the scanner. Scan the QR code from the volunteer's schedule email, or type the code. If the volunteer is more than 45 minutes early or 10 minutes late, it asks first: Check in anyway records the check-in with the timing. A slot's dialog has a Check In button too.

    Checked-in slots glow green. Past slots with nobody checked in pulse red.

    Ratings and notes. A slot's dialog can rate the volunteer Good, Bad or No-Show, and holds notes for that slot.

    Radios. On the Radios tab, Check Out a radio by scanning its barcode and choosing the volunteer, or typing a name. A radio registers itself the first time it's scanned. Scanning a radio someone else holds hands it over. To return radios, press Check In and scan each one. The tab lists what's out, what's available, and the full log.

    Security alerts¶

    The site watches its own audit log and emails a security alert when something looks wrong or important, for example:

    • many failed sign-ins from one address, or for one account
    • someone trying lots of different usernames
    • an account created, or its role changed
    • another person's password, passkeys or reset address changed
    • the volunteer email key replaced, or an export API key created
    • an organizer signing in from a network they haven't used in the last 30 days
    • requests to the organizer site or the volunteer pages that look like an attack (SQL or script injection, scanning tools), or one address piling up errors
    • input to the volunteer pages that the pages themselves would never send, such as a name with digits or a field the form doesn't have. Someone is calling the portal directly.

    Alerts go to the organizers ticked under Security alerts in Admin → Users who have an email address in Profile. At first that's only the first organizer account; any organizer can tick or untick anyone. If nobody qualifies, alerts are still recorded but not sent, and the Users and Audit Log screens say so in red. Each alert links to the audit log.

    One kind of alert comes at most once an hour for the same cause. Anything more in that hour is counted and sent as a "(continued)" summary when the hour is up, so a sustained attack is a trickle of mail, not a flood, and nothing goes unreported because it stopped.

    Treat what's in an alert as untrusted. Details such as a username can be whatever the attacker typed, and anything that looked like a link has been broken on purpose. Open the site yourself rather than following anything in the details.

    The audit log¶

    Admin → Audit Log (for every organizer) lists everything the site has recorded, newest first: sign-ins and failed attempts, account and role changes, schedule and volunteer changes, export API calls, attack patterns noticed in requests, and every alert. Filter by kind of event, severity, who, address, which site, or words in the message, and open a row to see its details. Alert emails link straight here.

    The card at the top shows who alerts reach, and warns if that's nobody. Send test alert emails everyone there now, to check the mail path end to end.

    Your own account¶

    Profile, under your name in the top right, changes your own password and email. Both need your current password. It's also where you add a passkey, so you can sign in without a password.

    If you forget your password, Send Reset Link on the sign-in page mails a link to the address on your account. An organizer can also set a new password for you under Admin → Users.

    After 5 wrong passwords in 15 minutes, the sign-in page stops accepting that username from your network for a while and says how long to wait. Waiting more is the only fix; more attempts don't help. A passkey or a reset link still works in the meantime, and so does signing in from another network. Successful sign-ins never count, so a check-in desk full of laptops on the venue's network can't lock itself out.
