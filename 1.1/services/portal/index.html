    Portal: the volunteer side¶

    The portal is where volunteers sign up, sign in and manage their own registration. It's a separate application from the scheduler: its own SQLite database, its own sessions, and no shared code. The only link between the two is the signed sync API, and traffic on it flows one way, from the scheduler to the portal.

    Code: portal/. Reference: portal.main and the modules beside it.

    Why it's separate¶

    The portal faces the internet; the scheduler sits behind an access gate. If the portal's host were fully compromised, the attacker would get volunteer names, email ciphertext and a copy of the schedule. They wouldn't get organizer accounts, notes, ratings, radio history, the scheduler's cookie secret, or a single readable email address. The portal can't decrypt addresses, and it has no way to call the scheduler.

    Layout¶

    Module What it does
    main The app and its startup. Runs one worker, since SQLite has one writer.
    config Settings from the environment. Refuses to start without the shared keys.
    db SQLite connections, migrations, the revision counter, portal_kv and the audit log.
    security Email HMAC and encryption, token hashing, request signing.
    ratelimit Per-address and per-client buckets in the database.
    mailer Sends through the relay, SMTP, or .eml files.
    routers/auth Sign-in links, sessions, and GET /api/portal/me.
    routers/signup The signup form, and changing, cancelling and rejoining.
    routers/sync The API the scheduler calls.
    routers/branding The title, logos and contact details (email, website, code of conduct) the scheduler pushed.
    signup_fields The questionnaire in force, and checking answers against it.
    content The organizer-editable landing page, questionnaire and invite email: defaults, checks, the sandboxed templates and the safe Markdown.

    The volunteer pages themselves are routes in the frontend under /apply/, and nginx sends /api/portal/* here.

    Signing in¶

    Volunteers have no passwords. They sign in with a magic link mailed to their address:

    1. request_link mails a single-use link, valid for 15 minutes, to a registered address.
    2. The link opens /apply/verify, which does nothing until the volunteer presses the button. Mail scanners fetch links, so a GET never spends a token.
    3. The button's POST spends the token in one conditional UPDATE, so two clicks can't both win, and starts a session: 12 hours, or 90 days with Remember this browser.

    Only SHA-256 hashes of tokens and session ids are stored. A copy of portal.db isn't enough to sign in as anyone.

    Link requests are rate-limited per address and per client, where an IPv6 client counts by its /64. Each request also takes at least 250 ms, so timing doesn't reveal whether an address is registered.

    What organizers can change¶

    The landing page, the questionnaire and the early-access invite email are edited in the scheduler's Volunteer Portal dialog and pushed here, where each push is checked again before anything is stored:

    • Templates (the landing page's Markdown and the invite) are Jinja, rendered in the sandbox and cut down further: variables from a fixed list, {% if %}, comparisons and a few text filters. No loops, set, includes, calls or attribute access.
    • Markdown is rendered with raw HTML and images off, and links only to web pages, email addresses and this site.
    • The questionnaire must be a shape the form and the server's validation understand.

    Until a part is saved, the portal uses its default, which is what the site said before this was editable.

    Signing up¶

    Signup is email first:

    1. Email. A registered address is told to sign in instead. Any other gets a signup link.
    2. The link gives a signup session, which can do exactly one thing: submit the form.
    3. About you, shifts, questions, review. The form submits the name, two preferred timeslots in order, any extra timeslots, and the questionnaire answers.
    4. The portal creates a pending volunteer with a p-… id and upgrades the session in place. An organizer approves them in the scheduler.

    Signup is open only when an organizer has opened it, the email public key is on the portal, and there are two non-overlapping timeslots with space. Otherwise the page shows it as closed or full. An early-access invite link gets one person past "closed", but never past "full". The invite is spent at submit, not when the email is typed.

    The two preferred timeslots can't overlap. Space is checked again inside the submit's transaction, so two people can't take the last place at once. remaining comes from the scheduler's snapshot, which includes any overbooking allowance, and the portal counts down from it until the next push.

    A volunteer's own page¶

    /apply/me shows the volunteer's status, their assigned shifts, their picks and their answers. While pending or approved, they can:

    • Change their shifts. The same rules as signup. A shift they already hold can be kept even when it reads as full. Dropping a shift they're assigned to is allowed: the page warns them, and the scheduler flags it for an organizer.
    • Change the answers marked editable (T-shirt size, dinner, flexibility).
    • Cancel. Their places go back at once. The picks are kept, so they can rejoin later.
    • Rejoin after cancelling, choosing shifts again. Their approval was never touched.
    • Sign out, here or everywhere.

    Email addresses¶

    Value What it is Who can reverse it
    email_hmac HMAC-SHA256 of the lowercased address, with EMAIL_HMAC_KEY nobody (but an address can be checked against it, with the key)
    email_enc RSA-OAEP ciphertext under the organizers' public key only private.pem, which never comes here

    A unique index on email_hmac is what prevents two signups for one address, even within the same second.

    Since the portal can't decrypt addresses, it only ever mails an address someone has just typed: a sign-in link, during their own request. The one exception is admin mail the scheduler asks for (organizer invites, password resets and early-access invites). Each is a fixed template whose link must point at this deployment's own site, and each is rate-limited. Every other message to volunteers is sent offline, by vol_mail.

    The sync API¶

    The scheduler signs every request with SYNC_SECRET: method, path, timestamp, nonce and a hash of the body. A timestamp more than 300 seconds off, or a nonce seen before, is refused. Every failure is the same bare 401. Every POST carries an op_id, and a repeat gets the stored result back, so a retry can't apply twice.

    Call What it does
    GET heartbeat, GET config Health, cursors, key fingerprints, settings
    GET volunteers?since= Volunteers changed since a cursor, with their picks
    POST schedule Replace the schedule snapshot
    POST volunteers Apply organizer decisions, and re-keys to a badge number
    POST branding, settings, signup_invites, announcements Replace each
    POST send_admin_mail Send one admin message
    POST content Replace the landing page, questionnaire and invite email, each checked here first
    POST purge_volunteers Delete volunteers outright, with their sessions and rate-limit counters
    POST email_index Fill in email_hmac for volunteers who have only ciphertext, from values made offline with the private key

    Data¶

    SQLite at /data/portal.db on the portal_data volume, with WAL and foreign keys on. The schema version is PRAGMA user_version, separate from the scheduler's, and the portal migrates itself on startup. Back it up with sqlite3 .backup, never cp. See Deployment → Backups.
